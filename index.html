    CASE STUDY - ELECTROCARDIOGRAPHY

    Sarah Mitchell, a 58-year-old woman, presents to her primary care physician with complaints of intermittent chest pain and shortness of breath over the past week. She describes the pain as a sharp discomfort in the center of her chest that comes on suddenly, particularly when she is walking briskly or climbing stairs. She mentions feeling fatigued more than usual and sometimes experiencing dizziness. Her medical history includes high blood pressure (hypertension) and a family history of heart disease. She is a non-smoker and maintains a relatively healthy diet.

    Sarah’s vital signs at the clinic are:

    • Blood pressure: 145/90 mmHg
    • Heart rate: 88 bpm
    • Respiratory rate: 18 breaths per minute
    • Temperature: 98.6°F
    • Oxygen saturation: 97%

    Given her symptoms and risk factors, her primary care physician suspects she may be at risk for coronary artery disease (CAD) and decides to order an electrocardiogram (ECG) to assess her heart’s electrical activity.

    2. ECG Procedure

    The students should describe the steps involved in performing an ECG:

    • Preparation: Patient positioning (typically supine), ensuring proper electrode placement (chest, arms, and legs), and explaining the procedure to reduce patient anxiety.
    • Electrode placement: Correct placement of 12-lead electrodes to get a comprehensive view of the heart’s electrical activity.
    • Recording and Interpretation: Discussing the ECG machine setup and how to interpret the different waves (P, QRS, T) and intervals (PR, QT).

    3. Interpretation of ECG Findings

    Sarah’s ECG shows:

    • Normal Sinus Rhythm with a heart rate of 88 bpm.
    • ST-segment depression in leads V4-V6.
    • T-wave inversion in the same leads (V4-V6).
    • Mild prolonged QT interval.

    Students should analyze these findings and explore:

    • What could ST-segment depression and T-wave inversion indicate?
    • Why is QT prolongation concerning?
    • How do these findings relate to Sarah's reported chest pain and risk factors?
